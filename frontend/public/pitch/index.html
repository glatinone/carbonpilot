CarbonPilot

An AI carbon decision engine. It turns fragmented operational data into prioritised, financially-aware decarbonisation actions — and every number it prints is traceable to a calculation engine, never to the language model.

Six screens, one loop: ingest, measure, diagnose, simulate, act, and price the same load against twenty regional grids.

The problem

Companies can already measure their carbon. They still cannot decide what to do about it.

  1. The data exists, the decision does not. Electricity, cloud, travel, fleet and procurement sit in CSVs and ERP exports. Aggregating them produces a number, not a plan.
  2. Reporting is a backward view. A footprint tells you where you were. Management asks what changes next, and what it will cost.
  3. Carbon and money are decided separately. The largest reduction is often the most expensive, and nobody can see the trade-off in one place.

What it does

It answers the question after the footprint.

  1. Ingest. A CSV is mapped to canonical activity types and scopes, and the interpretation is shown for confirmation. Nothing is written until a human has seen it.
  2. Diagnose. A robust median/MAD test finds the months that moved, then attributes each one to the activities that moved with it.
  3. Simulate. Interventions are applied to the stored baseline, and a constraint search explores combinations of them.
  4. Act. Recommendations are ranked on carbon × cost × effort, weighted by the confidence of their factors, then tracked to an owner.

The one architectural rule

The language model never produces a carbon number.

The engine in app/engine/ computes every kilogram. The agent chooses which analyses to run and explains the result, so its failure mode is “I could not find that” — never “here is a plausible number”.

How it works

An agent that orchestrates tools, over an engine that cannot be argued with.

The loop

question→ agent→ tool calls→ engine→ explanation
  • 13 tools, and the first step is forced to call one — the model cannot answer a quantitative question from its own head.
  • Retrieval over a cited corpus. A pure-stdlib BM25 ranker retrieves the methodology passages behind a claim, so “why is that factor defensible” is answered from a source of record rather than from recollection.
  • Every recommendation shows its arithmetic: the quantity, the factor, the factor’s source and year, and the uncertainty.

And it is verified

  • 138 tests asserting behaviour contracts, not snapshots: that a projection plus its saving equals the baseline, that the constraint search only ever returns scenarios that satisfy the constraints.
  • A preflight that fails on a working-looking key. A dead key produces no error — it produces a correct answer from the deterministic router while the UI still claims the model. That failure is invisible by construction, so the smoke test exists to make it visible.
  • One process. FastAPI serves the UI and the API on one port: no CORS layer, no second deployment, no vendor onboarding.

Watershed, Persefoni, Sweep and SINAI already do carbon accounting, AI data ingestion and scenario planning. We do not claim to be the first AI carbon platform; that claim is false and easy to disprove.

What is narrower and defensible: their AI is aimed at the input pipeline — extracting, mapping, cleaning. Ours is a contract about the output: the model cannot emit a number because the number does not come from the model.

Carbon reporting tells you where you are. CarbonPilot tells you what to do next.

Demonstration build: no compliance reporting (CSRD, SB 253, CDP), no supplier portal or ERP connectors, no multi-tenancy, curated point-estimate factors, and linear scenario maths. Stated here rather than discovered by a judge.
